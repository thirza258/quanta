# Financial news to company & stock analysis

The course appears immediately after **Security Analysis** at `/curriculum/news-to-markets`. Its 24 original lessons use new IDs `8.1`–`8.24`; all earlier course and lesson identifiers remain stable.

There are **12 hours of guided study**, **48 practice questions**, **six four-question checkpoints**, and a **24-question final exam**. Each 30-minute session budgets eight minutes for the lesson and primary reading, eight for a worksheet, and fourteen for a written interpretation and review. Exam time is additional. The final exam requires at least 80%; each unit checkpoint requires 75%.

| Unit | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Read and verify news | Facts versus claims, sources and corrections, timestamps, materiality, units, expectations |
| Company disclosures | Earnings bridges, cash conversion, guidance, KPI definitions, financing, dilution and corporate actions |
| Macroeconomic releases | Inflation/base effects, growth and revisions, rates/credit, rupiah exposures and Bank Indonesia |
| Industry transmission | Commodities, supply chains, pass-through, event-stage uncertainty, sector-specific economics |
| Stock-market interpretation | Reverse valuation, event returns, liquidity, second-order effects, scenarios and probability updates |
| Research workflow | Cited memos, supervised AI extraction, sentiment evaluation, decision review and a full capstone |

Each lesson includes an explanation, worked case, mathematical formula, optional executable Python worksheet, two practice questions, an assignment with a worked solution, and named references. Worksheets demonstrate case arithmetic and remain in cash in the price sandbox; their equity curves do not validate news-driven returns.

The [downloadable originals](../content/projects/news-to-markets/README.md) include a fictional company news packet with information-availability times, a memo template and self-assessment rubric, an English–Indonesian glossary, and a standard-library Python capstone calculation. In-app **Write in notebook** creates a lesson-linked assignment. ZIP and standalone downloads are available from the course page.

All companies, releases, forecasts, prices, and probabilities in the worked cases are fictional. Primary references include SEC, BLS, BEA, the Federal Reserve, Bank Indonesia, EIA, FINRA, and NYU Stern teaching material. Original explanations and short references are used; the project does not reproduce news articles or books. Attribution does not itself establish rights to reuse third-party material.

The capstone connects operating news to EBIT, FCFF, financing claims, value per share, price comparison, counterarguments, and review conditions. Its memo is self-assessed separately from automatic exam grading. There is no live news feed, automated news trading, causal-return guarantee, or investment credential.
