# Stock trading simulator

The new `/simulator` terminal implements PRD Section 6.3's first release. Open **Trading simulator** in navigation, or follow the practice link in beginner lessons 0.4, 0.5, 0.7, and 0.8. The research lab's small execution calculator remains a separate teaching tool.

## First trade

1. Choose a stock using the ticker search or watchlist. Inspect the chart, change timeframe, or add indicators.
2. Open **Buy**, enter whole shares and the order type, then preview the estimated value, fees, reservations, and resulting position. **Place buy order** explicitly submits it.
3. Advance replay. Accepted orders wait for a subsequent eligible bar; acceptance itself does not change holdings.
4. Open **Positions** to reduce or close through a prefilled sell ticket. Review fills, fees, P&L, and cash entries in the bottom tabs.
5. Add a trade thesis and lesson to the journal. Export each table as CSV or the run, settings, and journal as JSON.

The chart supports candles, OHLC bars, line, pan/zoom, crosshair, fit/latest, volume, SMA, EMA, Wilder RSI, and MACD. Overlay values are served by the API alongside the bars they belong to; the browser draws them rather than computing them. The left toolbar provides trend/horizontal lines, rectangles, text, and price measurements. Objects can also be created and edited using the **Objects** form, including dates, prices, color, label, lock, delete, and undo/redo. Drawings belong to their symbol and run. Dragging an order price opens an amendment confirmation; its form is also available through **Open orders → Edit**.

The symbol button opens a command entry. Typing a ticker selects it; typing a function code moves to that view; typing both does each in turn, so `NVRA FA` opens Novara Microsystems' generated accounts. The codes are `GP` chart, `N` news, `MOV` desk, `FA` or `DES` company financials, `RV` or `PEER` the peer table, `CAL`, `ECO` or `EARN` the calendar, `PORT` performance, `POS` positions, `OMON` open orders, `BLOT` trade history, `W` watchlist, `BUY` and `SELL` tickets, and `HELP` the execution model. They are also listed as buttons in the dialog, applied to the current symbol.

Desktop panels resize by dragging their separators or using arrow keys while a separator has focus. The right sidebar holds Watchlist, News, Desk, Trade, and Drawings. The bottom trading panel holds Positions, Open orders, Order history, Trade history, Account history, Company, Performance, and Calendar. A headline strip above the grid carries the most recently released simulated items; choosing one selects that company and opens the news panel. At tablet widths the right panel becomes a drawer; the toolbar toggles it. At mobile widths, Chart, Watchlist, News, Trade, and Portfolio tabs accompany persistent Buy/Sell entry buttons, and the sidebar tabs remain available. Dense tables scroll inside the trading panel. Light/dark themes follow the learning workspace. An optional walkthrough explains the first trade and execution model.

## Market data and clock

The default run starts with $100,000 of virtual cash and 32 fictional companies across 12 sectors. A generated run has no end. Sessions are produced a block of 21 at a time as the replay clock reaches them, each block continuing from the closing prices of the one before, so the market keeps generating for as long as a learner keeps advancing. Sessions are 390 one-minute bars, 09:30–16:00 America/New_York, starting January 6, 2025; exchange holidays are omitted. The first 500 observations are available as warm-up. One block beyond the cursor is always kept ahead, so the next bar, the next headline and the next scheduled report already exist. This is a deterministic teaching market, not a reconstruction or live feed. Every company, ticker, sector, and price is invented, and no generated series is labeled as observed market history.

A block is a pure function of the seed and its own first session, so a block generated after a server restart is identical to the same block generated during the original replay, and a stored cursor always resolves to the same bar. A run only holds the sessions it has actually reached: roughly 5 MB of bars per simulated month, plus its headlines. Because the clock never runs out, replay never reports itself complete and orders are never refused for want of a next bar.

Prices come from a factor model rather than independent random walks. Each bar's return combines one market factor, a factor for the company's sector, company-specific noise sized to that company's stated volatility, and the shocks released by the run's own simulated news. The news schedule is generated first; the price engine then applies each item's modeled shock to the return, volatility, and volume processes on the bar the item is released, decaying over the bars that follow. A macroeconomic release moves every company through its beta, a sector item moves that sector's members through their sector loading, and a company item moves one symbol. Volatility clusters, the intraday profile is U-shaped, busiest at the open and firm again into the close, and a session's first bar carries most of its move as an overnight gap. Not every candle has a story: routine items carry no modeled shock, and the noise terms move prices on their own. What the model does guarantee is the converse, that a released shock lands on the bar its headline is dated, so a learner can look up what the tape said when a move happened.

A new run may request a market seed or omit it. When it is omitted, the run derives its seed from its own identifier, so every new paper account gets a different market, while an idempotent retry of the same request returns the original run instead of rolling a new one. Supplying a seed reproduces a market exactly: bars, headlines, the reporting calendar, and the generated statements are all pure functions of the stored seed, so two runs on the same seed replay identically. The new-run dialog labels the field **Market seed (optional)** and shows **Automatic** as its placeholder.

Runs created before this universe existed keep their original six symbols and their original 6,000-bar generator, unchanged, so their stored cursors, orders, fills, and positions still resolve against the same bars. They remain tradable, reviewable, and exportable, but they have no newsroom and no generated statements.

Installed historical datasets come from the existing licensed CSV → Parquet importer described in the README. The terminal snapshots an imported dataset into its database, so removing or replacing an import cannot rewrite an existing run. Historical imports contain daily OHLCV; intraday choices are disabled. Daily bar timestamps label completed sessions at 16:00 ET; they do not assert the exact intraday time of a modeled fill. The initial warm-up is at most 31 daily bars, retaining at least one replay step.

Only observations at or before the run's cursor leave the server, and a chart reads a window of them rather than the whole run: `limit` sets how many bars come back, defaulting to 1,500 and capped at 5,000, with `from_bar` naming the oldest observation in the window. Because a generated run has no end, this is what keeps a chart request flat as the clock advances — a full-history read reached 7.3 MB by the hundred-and-fiftieth session, where a window stays near 275 KB. Overlays are calculated across a 400-bar margin before the window and then trimmed with the bars, so resizing the window does not change the values a learner sees. Coarser intervals are bucketed directly from the stored columns, so an hourly chart deep into a run costs about as much as a fresh one. Coarser intervals aggregate those observations, including the observed portion of an unfinished aggregate bar. Indicators are computed in the API from that same observed series, with missing warm-up values returned as `null`. Quotes, positions, and fills share one server clock. Play, speed, and next-bar advance it; pause allows order entry and cancellation without advancing prices. Advance accepts 1 to 1,950 observations, one modeled week, so the replay bar can jump fifteen minutes, an hour, a session, or a week at once; a daily historical replay jumps by one session, week, month, or quarter. A generated market produces whatever sessions the jump lands in, so a run can be carried through simulated months and years without stopping. Moving back is accomplished by creating a new run, preserving the previous run for review/export.

Historical corporate-action treatment is explicitly **undeclared** for existing imports. Prices are used as supplied: this engine adds no split adjustments, dividends, or other corporate-action cash flows. Import suitably adjusted data and interpret returns using its provider's conventions. No historical files or live-data credentials ship with the application; unavailable imports remain unavailable.

## Simulated newsroom

Generated runs carry a simulated newsroom. Every wire, broker, analyst, statistics body, index, and headline in it is invented: stories carry sources such as Quanta Newswire and Harbor Point Wire, company filings arrive from Simulated Exchange Filings, macroeconomic releases come from bodies such as the Bureau of Price Statistics and the Reserve Board, and the market index is the QX-200. No real news organization, agency, central bank, broker, or issuer is described. The run snapshot and every news, statements, and analytics response carry the run's disclosure string, and each item carries a summary stating that it is simulated.

Roughly 70 items are scheduled per session, and the tape continues for as long as the run does. Each session receives two macroeconomic releases, an opening and a closing market wrap, sector items for about half of the twelve sectors, and, for each company, one draw of market-moving items and a second draw of routine chatter, both scaled by how newsworthy that company is. Company items fall into earnings, guidance, analyst, product, regulatory, legal, supply, capital, deal, and leadership categories. Chatter carries no score and exists to keep the tape realistic.

### How an item moves a price

Every item carries a signed integer score, and the rule is fixed rather than inferred: **one point is one basis point**, so a +250 point headline is a modeled +2.50% move. Of that, 58% lands on the bar the item is dated and the rest drifts over the bars that follow, while the same item raises modeled volatility and traded volume around itself. A company item applies its score to that company, a sector item to every member of its sector through their sector loading, and a macroeconomic release to every company through its beta.

Scores are drawn when the block is generated, from ranges fixed per category: an analyst note runs 40 to 190 points, guidance 160 to 550, a deal 300 to 1,100, and an earnings print is scored from its own surprise, about 62 points per percentage point, capped at 1,400. Each is then scaled by the company's own volatility, so a utility and a quantum-computing venture do not move alike on the same kind of headline. The published impact tier is derived from the published score — high at 400 points, medium at 120 — so a reader can check one against the other. Nothing is computed by a model at request time; the number shown beside a headline is the number the price engine applied.

Every company reports once a quarter, for as long as the run continues. Its first print falls between the fourth and the twenty-first session, so a new account meets earnings season early, and the cycle repeats every 63 sessions from there. Prints land on a session open, so the result arrives with the gap it causes, and no company has reported at the opening cursor. A broker preview appears just under two sessions earlier and carries only the expected earnings per share, never the result. The next scheduled date is published even when that quarter is further out than the market has been generated; the street's estimate appears once the quarter has been scheduled.

News tone is impact-weighted: the signed weight of released items divided by their total weight, which keeps the score between -1 and 1 and independent of how chatty the tape is. The news endpoint returns tone for the last modeled session, the last modeled week, and the whole market. The screener's per-company tone excludes macroeconomic releases, because an item that moves every company the same way cannot rank one company against another.

Nothing is released ahead of the cursor. Items dated beyond it are skipped, and tone counts only bars the run has already observed. A symbol filter returns that company's own items plus the sector and macroeconomic items that move it, which is what a desk would watch. Imported historical prices never receive invented news: for a run replaying an import, and for a run created before this universe, the news endpoint returns `available: false` with a notice instead of items.

## Generated company accounts

Every fictional company has generated quarterly statements: an income statement, a balance sheet, and a cash-flow statement. Eight quarters are already on file when a run opens, ending Q3 2024, and a ninth, Q4 2024, is released during the run by the newsroom item that reports it. Every figure is invented. Nothing is derived from real company accounts, and no filing or reported result is described.

A company's structure is stable across runs: shares outstanding, the sector margin profile, capital intensity, leverage, payout, tax rate, employee count, and the valuation it starts from are a fixed function of its ticker, so the same fictional company is recognizably the same business in every run. What varies with the run seed is how it performs, because each quarter carries its own growth, margin, and earnings surprise. Five companies are modeled as development-stage: they run negative operating margins, pay no dividend, and are priced on sales rather than earnings.

The statements are built to tie together. Equity is the residual of assets and liabilities, so the balance sheet balances, and each quarter's closing cash is the previous quarter's cash plus that quarter's modeled operating, investing, and financing flows. A company that would otherwise fall below a working cash minimum raises the shortfall instead of finding it: the borrowing appears on the cash-flow statement as external financing and on the balance sheet as short-term debt, so the roll-forward holds exactly.

Before a print, only the expectation is published: the quarter's label, the bar it lands on, how far away that is, and the consensus earnings and revenue. The reported figures leave the server only once the cursor reaches that bar. Consensus is stored with the released quarter, so the surprise can be computed from published numbers alone.

Two rules keep the print from being solvable in advance. The earnings and revenue estimates each carry their own dispersion rather than being an exact inverse of the reported figures, and the parameters that generate a quarter — margin profile, growth, leverage, capital intensity, payout, tax rate — are never published. The company page carries descriptive facts only: shares outstanding, employees, fiscal year end, and whether the company is development-stage. A surprise is also stated as a distance from the reported figure rather than a ratio to it, so a beat is above consensus whether the company earns or loses money.

A valuation block is computed from the released quarters and the current mark: P/E, P/S, P/B, EV/sales, EV/EBITDA, free-cash-flow yield, dividend yield and payout, gross, operating, and net margins, return on equity and assets, revenue and earnings growth, current and quick ratios, working capital, debt/equity, net debt/EBITDA, interest coverage, book value per share, and Graham's net current asset value and Graham number, the same arithmetic the value courses teach. A ratio with a zero or non-positive denominator is returned as `null` rather than a misleading figure.

Imported historical runs, and runs created before this universe, have no generated statements: the fundamentals endpoint returns 422. Attaching invented accounts to a real imported price series would present generated data as a real company's reported results.

## Desk analytics

Indicators, screening, risk statistics, and portfolio performance are computed in the API from the bars the run has already observed. The client renders what the API returns; it does not recompute any of it. Nothing beyond the cursor is read, so no displayed figure can depend on a price the learner has not seen.

Chart overlays are requested with the market bars. `indicators=SMA:20,RSI:14,MACD:12:26:9` returns one value per returned bar, aligned to that series, with warm-up positions as `null`. SMA is a rolling mean, EMA is seeded with the first full simple average, RSI uses Wilder smoothing, and MACD returns its line, signal, and histogram. At most eight specifications are accepted per request, and each period is clamped to between 2 and 200. An unrecognized specification is omitted rather than failing the request.

The desk endpoint returns a screener over every company in the run: gainers, losers, and turnover leaders, each row carrying last price, change against the previous session's close, volume and relative volume, the window range, annualized realized volatility, and news tone; a sector table averaging those moves; and market breadth. Change is measured against the same previous-session close the watchlist uses, so the two panels never disagree, and a company appears among the gainers or the losers but never both. It also returns portfolio performance: the account marked to market on every observed bar, an equity curve sampled to about 180 points plus the latest bar, opening and current equity, total return, maximum drawdown, realized and unrealized P&L, average exposure, trade count, win rate, profit factor, average win and loss, and per-symbol attribution.

The calendar endpoint lists what is scheduled: the economic releases in the run's own calendar and the quarter each company is due to report. An entry the cursor has not reached carries its appointment only — the release's name and its date, and for a company its period and published consensus. The outcome, the headline, and the reported figures attach once the bar that prints them has been observed. An optional `ahead` bound keeps the board short. A run without a newsroom has no calendar and returns 422.

The statements page also carries a peer table: the other companies in the same sector, each priced at its own mark and measured on the quarters it has itself reported, with sector medians that skip the companies for which a ratio is undefined. Because companies report on different dates, the columns are not always measured over the same periods, and the table says so.

The analytics endpoint covers one company: SMA 20 and 50, EMA 20, RSI 14, and MACD; short- and long-window realized volatility; beta and correlation against the equal-weighted average of the run's companies; session, week, and since-open momentum; 20-bar, window, and run ranges; last and average volume; and its session and week news tone. Statistical windows are measured in the run's own observations, and the bar counts travel with the response: a minute market measures over a session and a week, while a daily replay, which has one observation per session, measures over a month and a quarter and annualizes by trading days rather than minutes. Beta is `null` for an imported replay, which holds a single instrument and therefore no market to measure against. Both endpoints serve imported historical runs, without the tone blocks, which are `null` when a run has no newsroom.

## Practice missions

A mission is a lesson's exercise carried into the terminal: a fixed seed, a fixed starting cursor, a named company, a short brief, and three objectives. Because the seed is fixed, everyone attempting the same mission meets the same market, and the same objectives can be judged the same way.

Lessons that have one link straight to it. Lesson 0.4 opens `/simulator?mission=first-order&lesson=0.4`, which preselects the mission in the new-run dialog and tags the ticket with the lesson. Six missions ship: placing a first limit order with a written reason, sizing a position under a stated cap, holding a company through its quarterly print, entering only after a scored headline, attaching a protective stop while the position is still calm, and trading a week inside the charter the ethics course asks learners to write.

**Objectives describe process, never profit.** They ask what was done and recorded — an entry priced with a limit, a thesis on every order, a position kept under a share of opening equity, a session advanced after the print — and none of them reads P&L. That is deliberate: the courses repeat that a profitable breach of a rule is still a breach, and a loss taken while following the rules is not evidence the rules were wrong, so a mission that paid out for a rule violation would teach the opposite of the lesson it serves. A position cap is judged on the largest holding the run ever carried, so closing an oversized position afterwards does not undo the breach.

Every objective is decided on the server from the run's own stored state — its orders, fills, positions, ledger and cursor, and the newsroom behind them — so progress is the same on any device, survives a reload, and cannot be claimed by the browser. `GET /runs/{run_id}/mission` returns the brief and each objective with a plain-language note on how far it has come; a run started without a mission returns 404. `GET /missions` lists the catalog for the lesson pages, which hold their own copy of the lesson-to-mission map so a lesson renders without waiting on the simulator; a test compares the two maps and fails if either side gains a mission the other does not have.

## Execution profile: cash-whole-shares-v1

- Long-only, whole-share, USD cash account with immediate settlement. Default commission is $1 per order, slippage 2 bps, spread 2 bps, and volume participation 1%. New-run configuration changes these assumptions explicitly.
- Orders entered after a completed bar first become eligible on the next bar. Market orders use its opening price with adverse slippage plus half-spread. Buy reservations round estimates upward to cents; ledger and fill arithmetic use Decimal and cent rounding.
- Buy orders reserve their remaining estimated notional and unpaid commission. Sell orders reserve remaining shares. Execution rechecks cash, reserved orders, proceeds, and fees; gaps cannot create borrowing. A gap may partially fill an affordable quantity and cancel its unaffordable remainder. A sale whose commission cannot be covered is rejected.
- Passive limit orders require trading strictly through the limit. An opening price at or better than the limit is eligible, subject to liquidity. Limit fills never breach the limit. A stop-market triggers on the high/low and gaps at the worse opening/trigger price, before costs. A stop-limit triggers on one bar and cannot fill before a later bar; this conservative rule avoids assuming an unknown intrabar path.
- All orders for a symbol share `floor(bar volume × participation)` shares of capacity, in FIFO arrival order. There is no implied Level 2 book. Partial fills preserve quantity and charge the flat commission only on the first fill. Cancellation releases only the unfilled reservation.
- DAY orders entered within a session expire at its boundary. Orders entered after its final bar are valid for the next modeled session, including daily replay. GTC orders remain until filled/canceled or the run is archived. End-of-replay never force-liquidates positions.
- Average cost includes entry fees. Partial exits allocate weighted-average basis to cents; the final exit consumes any remaining basis. Realized P&L deducts exit fees. Every fill produces an account ledger entry; unrealized P&L marks remaining holdings to the latest available close.

## Persistence and recovery

`TradingRun` stores account ownership, an optimistic version, and transactional order/fill/position/cash/journal state. `TradingDataset` stores immutable imported bars. Both tables are added during normal database initialization, without rewriting learning state. SQLite supports local development; Compose uses PostgreSQL.

Signed-in runs are owned by the existing authenticated account. Guests use a separate signed, HttpOnly, browser-scoped cookie valid for 30 days; their preview is also server-backed. Losing that cookie loses access to the guest preview. Guest and account trading balances never migrate or merge during sign-in/out. Simulator storage and pending drafts use their own namespaces, separate from lesson/exam progress and experiments.

Every write carries a stable UUID and expected version. An identical retry returns confirmed state; a reused identifier with different content or an outdated new action is rejected. The ledger and request record commit together. Reset also competes on the previous run's version, preventing an order from being committed to an account after its reset succeeds.

The browser saves an uncertain action and ticket locally. Network failures, server errors, and version conflicts pause replay. **Reconnect & retry action** first reloads authoritative state, then retries the same UUID. Unconfirmed trades are never inferred locally. New-run requests also retain their configuration and UUID until acknowledged. Old runs remain reviewable but cannot accept new economic actions.

Chart preferences save to the account and a device cache; unsaved local changes survive reload. Confirmed account changes refresh on reconnect and during idle polling. Layout preferences are shared across devices; the most recently saved layout wins. Financial writes always require the current version. Limits: 2,000 orders, 20,000 mutations, and 200 KB of chart preferences per run; export and start another run when reached.

## HTTP interface

All paths below start with `/api/v1/simulator`. Authentication uses the existing session cookie or separate guest cookie. GET and write responses are private/no-store. Interactive schemas are available at `/docs` on the API.

| Method | Path | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GET | Base path `/api/v1/simulator` | Current run, owned run summaries, available symbols/imports |
| POST | `/runs` | Create/reset; UUID plus mode, seed or historical symbol, and cost/liquidity configuration |
| GET | `/runs/{run_id}` | Owned account snapshot |
| GET | `/runs/{run_id}/market?symbol=NVRA&interval=1m&limit=1500&indicators=SMA:20` | A window of observed bars, plus any requested overlays computed from exactly those bars |
| GET | `/runs/{run_id}/news?symbol=NVRA&limit=60` | Simulated headlines already released at the cursor, newest first, each with its score and impact-weighted tone |
| GET | `/runs/{run_id}/fundamentals?symbol=NVRA` | Generated statements already reported at the cursor, plus consensus for the next print and a valuation block |
| GET | `/runs/{run_id}/calendar?ahead=1950` | Scheduled economic releases and company results; outcomes only once observed |
| GET | `/runs/{run_id}/desk` | Screener, sector table, breadth, and portfolio performance over observed bars |
| GET | `/runs/{run_id}/analytics?symbol=NVRA` | Indicators, risk, momentum, liquidity, and news tone for one company |
| POST | `/runs/{run_id}/orders` | Place market, limit, stop, or stop_limit; side buy/sell, integer quantity, DAY/GTC |
| POST | `/runs/{run_id}/advance` | Advance 1–1,950 observations, up to one modeled week |
| POST | `/runs/{run_id}/orders/{order_id}/cancel` | Cancel unfilled remainder |
| POST | `/runs/{run_id}/orders/{order_id}/amend` | Confirm changed limit/stop prices |
| PUT | `/runs/{run_id}/preferences` | Save chart settings |
| PUT | `/runs/{run_id}/orders/{order_id}/journal` | Save thesis/lesson on the order and its fills |

Every read is bounded by the run's cursor. `market` returns bars at or before it and computes its overlays from exactly those bars; it also accepts `cursor` to pin an earlier point, capped at the run's own cursor. `news` returns items dated at or before it, takes an optional `symbol` and a `limit` of 1 to 200 that defaults to 60, and returns `available: false` with a notice rather than an error when the run has no newsroom. `fundamentals` requires `symbol` and returns only quarters already reported, reducing an upcoming print to its label, date, and consensus; it returns 422 when the run has no newsroom, because a statements page has nothing to show without one. `desk` takes no symbol and reads the same observed slice for every company. `analytics` requires `symbol` and returns 404 for a symbol the run does not carry. `calendar` reduces anything still ahead of the cursor to its name, its date and, for results, the published consensus. The run snapshot and the news, fundamentals, desk, and analytics responses all carry the run's own provenance line, and it describes that run rather than the simulator in general: a generated market names fictional companies, a run created before this universe records that its fictional price paths are printed against real company names, and an imported replay records that its prices are used as supplied and that nothing is generated for it. Generated runs also store a provenance string in their configuration recording that their prices came from the run's simulated news on a weekday-only calendar.

All mutations within a run require `id` (UUID) and `version` (integer). Prices are decimal strings with at most two fractional digits. Client balances, execution prices, and P&L are not accepted. Orders also accept `note` and `lesson`. The same service can support a future Python client without reimplementing financial logic.

See [verification results](VERIFICATION.md) for acceptance coverage and local performance measurements; the recorded performance sample was taken against the previous six-symbol universe and has not been re-run.
