# Trading ethics & market conduct

The course appears at `/curriculum/trading-ethics`, immediately after **Trading & market fundamentals** and before **The intelligent & careful investor**. Its 16 lessons use new identifiers `12.1`–`12.16`; every earlier course and lesson identifier is unchanged.

It is placed early deliberately. Most of the hard moments in trading are not calculations but situations where the profitable action and the right action point in different directions, and a learner should settle whose interest a trade serves before learning how to value a security or automate an order.

## Four units

| Unit | Lessons | Covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duty, conflict and disclosure | 12.1–12.4 | Whose interest a decision serves, materiality and timing of disclosure, inducements and total cost, personal trading and client priority |
| Market integrity | 12.5–12.8 | Material non-public information, quotations that are not bona fide, promotion and the pump-and-dump, best execution and order handling |
| Conduct in automated and quantitative work | 12.9–12.12 | Accountability for a system that trades, honest backtests and the size of a search, data provenance and licensing, performance claims |
| Care, refusal and record | 12.13–12.16 | Risk capacity against tolerance, refusing an instruction that fails arithmetic or authority, escalation and records, the written charter |

Each lesson runs 30 minutes: 10 minutes reading the principle and the rule that expresses it, 8 minutes on a calculation, and 12 minutes adding a rule to a personal charter. Eight hours of planned study; exam time is additional.

## Do and do not

Every lesson ends with an explicit **Do and do not** block, and each rule is written to be checkable after the fact: a behaviour, a threshold, and an observable that shows whether the rule was followed. *No position above 5% of equity, verified from the end-of-day position file* is a rule; *manage risk carefully* is a preference. Lesson 12.16 assembles the course into a personal trading charter of six to ten such rules, with a review cadence and a decision, made in advance, about what a breach obliges you to do.

The course states repeatedly that a profitable breach is still a breach, and that a loss suffered while following the rules is not evidence the rules were wrong. Separating decision quality from outcome is the habit the charter is built to protect.

## Calculations

Conduct is taught through arithmetic rather than exhortation, because a number is harder to argue away. The lessons compute the annual cost of a conflicted recommendation, the portfolio weight that triggers a disclosure, total cost of ownership including turnover, a blackout window, the revenue effect that makes information material, an order-to-trade ratio against a desk norm, the days of average volume a promoter must sell, implementation shortfall against the decision price, the point at which a cumulative loss limit binds, a multiple-testing adjusted threshold and the probability of a false positive across a search, the hindsight introduced by a revised data series, a net-of-fee performance figure, risk capacity against a dated obligation, the shortfall a Ponzi structure must fund from new deposits, and a position limit and its breach.

## Sourcing and boundaries

Every firm, person, price and amount in the scenarios is invented, including the rupiah figures. The references are primary sources cited for the principle being described, not claims that any case in this course occurred: the CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct, SEC Regulation Best Interest and its insider-trading investor publication, FINRA Rules 2010, 2020, 3110, 5210 and 5310 and its advertising-regulation guidance, the SEC market-access rule and whistleblower programme, ESMA's market-integrity pages, the GIPS standards, and Investor.gov fraud and fee material.

Each lesson also carries an original application built on one of the curriculum's 43 shared case anchors, which supply the documented context with their own sourced references and boundary statements. The 16 lessons use 16 distinct anchors.

The course is education, not legal, compliance or investment advice. Obligations differ by jurisdiction, licence and role, and the course says so in the prerequisites, the reading guide and every lesson's reading note. Learners are told to check their own regulator's rules before acting.

## Assessment

32 practice questions across the lessons, four four-question unit checkpoints, and a 16-question final drawing one transfer question from every lesson. All 64 questions are distinct. Checkpoints pass at 75%, the final at 80%, and both are open-book, untimed and retryable.
